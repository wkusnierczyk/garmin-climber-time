ROLE
You compose one product hero image from renders of smartwatches supplied to you.
You are a photographer and retoucher arranging real objects, not an illustrator
drawing new ones.

INPUTS
Four images. Each is a front-on render of the same watch model, differing only in
what its screen shows. Treat each as a separate physical watch.
The screens show a watch face called Climber Time: a climbing wall with a tiny
stick-figure climber on the right half, and the time on the left half.

OUTPUT
A single image, exactly twice as wide as it is tall, at the largest resolution you
can produce and no smaller than 2880x1440. A flat pure white background, no
gradient, no vignette, no visible surface. No border, no frame, no caption.
Keep every watch inside the central area that is twice as wide as it is tall, with
a little clear white at its edges, so the image can be trimmed to an exact 2:1
without touching a watch.

INVARIANTS — these outrank every layout rule below
1. Each screen must stay a believable screenshot of this watch face. Reproduce each
   screen's layout, colours and content as closely as you can; you may re-render a
   screen to suit its viewpoint, but never change what the face is showing:
   - The left half of the screen is pure black. On it, the time as four digits with
     no colon (for example 1007): the first two digits white, the last two in the
     route colour. Under the minutes, two small square tags side by side: the
     grade, a digit from 4 to 8 in dark on a filled box of the route colour; and
     the skill, one letter B, I or E in the route colour inside an outlined box of
     the route colour with a black inside.
   - The right half of the screen is a dark slate climbing wall with a faint grid
     of dots, small climbing holds of one colour (the route colour) and a few dim
     holds of other colours, and exactly one small white stick-figure climber with
     a round head. Where the input shows a thin white rope from the top of the wall
     to the climber, keep it; where it does not, do not add one.
   - Each screen has its own route colour (pink, blue, red); keep each one exactly.
   - No digits anywhere on any screen except the four-digit time and the one grade
     digit. No other text.
   - One input is the always-on state: an otherwise black screen with a dim grey
     time (1007) and faint coloured dots on the right. It has no wall, no climber
     and no tags; it must stay that way. Do not add a wall, a climber or colour to it.
   Never add UI, icons, complications, notifications, dates or text that is not in
   the input.
2. The hardware stays this watch model: case shape and depth, bezel markings, button
   count and placement, strap type, material and colour.
3. Exactly four watches. Never add, duplicate, merge or drop one. Each input appears
   exactly once.
4. Nothing else in the frame: no text, logos, captions, watermarks, hands, wrists,
   packaging, studio props, or reflections of a photographer.

LAYOUT
5. Scatter them; do not line them up in a row, a grid, or an arc.
6. OVERLAP — at most 25% of any watch may be hidden by another. Watches may touch
   and slightly tuck behind one another. No screen is ever partly covered: every
   screen is fully visible, whole, unobstructed.
7. SIZE — the largest watch is at most 2x the linear size of the smallest. They read
   as the same object at different distances, not as different products.
8. ROTATION — these are real three-dimensional watches photographed from different
   angles, not flat pictures. Each watch is turned independently, within these limits:
   - roll (spin in the picture plane, 12 o'clock leaving vertical): up to ±15°
   - pitch (top tipping away from or toward the viewer): up to ±30°
   - yaw (left or right edge turning away): up to ±35°
   At ±15° a watch is clearly tilted but still read head-on; at ±40° you see well
   down its side wall and the case depth, lugs and crown come into view. At least
   three of the four watches show visible case depth: the side wall of the case, the
   lugs and the thickness of the strap. Draw a different combination for each watch;
   do not give them all the same attitude, and do not leave any of them square to
   the camera. Each screen is foreshortened with its watch, as glass seen at an angle.
9. Every watch lies fully inside the canvas with at least 40 px clear of each edge.
   Straps may run off the edge; cases and screens may not.
10. Fill the canvas evenly. No empty quadrant, no watches crowded into one band.

REALISM
11. One light source for the whole image, from above and slightly left, soft and
    broad. Every highlight, shadow and reflection agrees with it, including on
    watches turned away from the camera. The glass of each screen carries a faint,
    consistent reflection of that light, without hiding the screen's content.
12. A soft contact shadow under each watch, consistent with that light and with how
    far the watch is from the surface. This is what makes them one photograph.
13. Straps rest naturally with their own curve and thickness; they are not flat
    cut-outs.

BEFORE YOU RETURN THE IMAGE, CHECK
- Count the watches. If the number is not four, remove the extras or add the missing
  one before returning. This has gone wrong before.
- Count the climbers: exactly one on each of the three screens with a wall, none on
  the always-on screen.
- Read every screen. The only digits are the four-digit time and the one grade
  digit; the only letter is the skill letter (B, I or E).
- The always-on screen has no wall, no climber and no tags.
- Each watch matches one input, each screen faithful to it, its route colour kept.
- Every watch shows real depth; none is a flat front-on picture.
- No screen partly hidden. No watch hidden by more than 25%.
- Largest/smallest ratio within 2x. Rotations within the stated limits.
- All watches inside the canvas with the stated margin. No empty quadrant.
- One consistent light. No added text or objects.

IF CONSTRAINTS CONFLICT, in this order: screen fidelity > the count > no covered
screen > overlap limit > margins > rotation limits > size limits > even fill.
Never resolve a conflict by dropping or duplicating a watch.
